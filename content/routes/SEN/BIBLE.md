# Senna — character bible (route `SEN`)

**Owner request (2026-10-25):** "can you implement a character route who is like mao mao from the apothecary diaries?" The owner chose:

- **Role:** a princess-apothecary.
- **Name:** Senna.

**Originality.** Senna is an **original character in the deadpan-apothecary mould**. Nothing is borrowed from that work: not its names, places or plot, not its supporting characters, and not its signature look. That means:

- no freckles;
- no servant's clothes;
- no rear-palace setting;
- no masked noble admirer;
- no self-experimenting with poison.

Write her from this bible, not from memory of anything else.

**All ages.** The game is for everyone:

- Poisons and remedies are knowledge and safety. She identifies harm in order to prevent it.
- She never poisons anyone, and never tests anything on herself or on anyone else.
- Injuries are mild: splinters, sprains, a burnt thumb, a bee sting, a cold.
- Romance stays at banter, holding hands, sitting close and, in the epilogue, one forehead touch at most.
- No alcohol, no blood, no scars on show.
- All skin is covered by clothing in every description.

## Who she is

- **Princess Senna of Hollowmere**, second daughter of a small, damp hill kingdom. It is famous for its physic gardens and its weather.
- Her mother, the Queen of Hollowmere, sent her to the courtship season to "become presentable and find someone who will put up with her".
- Senna arrived, found the palace infirmary understaffed, and moved in with four trunks of dried herbs.
- She has not attended a single ball. She has, so far, treated forty-one sprains.
- **Age:** an adult (early twenties). Never state a number.

**Look.** All covered, all ages. This is for writers' descriptions and for the art.

- Honey-blonde hair in a messy low bun, with a pencil and a sprig of rosemary pushed through it.
- Round wire spectacles, usually pushed up on her head, which she then searches for.
- Hazel-grey eyes.
- A high-collared, long-sleeved plum dress.
- Over it, a canvas apothecary's apron covered in labelled pockets: twine, vials, a magnifying lens, a notebook, a bag of boiled sweets she says are "for patients".
- Gloves when handling plants.

**Voice.**

- **Deadpan, precise, short.** Declarative sentences. She does not do small talk; she does diagnosis.
- **Compliments are symptoms.** "Your pulse is elevated. Either you like me or you took the stairs." When she's flustered, she clinically narrates her own symptoms.
- **She lights up for plants and poisons, and only for them.** That's when the sentences get longer, the facts pile up and she forgets to be guarded. "Foxglove. Pretty. Will stop your heart. I've never trusted anything more."
- **The running gag is "Don't eat that."** It works on garnishes, berries, mushrooms, a suspicious pastry and once a decorative soap.
- **She is unimpressed by titles**, including her own. She calls the Royal Physician "Doctor Leeches" (his name is Dorrance) and is correct about everything he is wrong about.
- **Kindness is shown, never claimed.** She treats the stable hands for free, scolds people into taking their medicine, and keeps boiled sweets for frightened children. If thanked, she changes the subject to dosage.
- **Contractions:** she uses them normally, in short sentences. She is crisp, not formal: "Don't." / "It's fine." / "That's hemlock water dropwort. Put it down slowly."
- **Vocabulary:** latin plant names when excited, plain words otherwise. Never therapy-speak. She would say "Stop that," not "I need you to respect my boundaries."
- **Other voices:** Tavri jokes and performs; Nerelle is wide-eyed and literal about land customs; Kaedra is formal and careful. Senna is blunt and a little dry, and the warmth is underneath. Don't let her become any of them.

## What she wants, and what she's afraid of

- **The fear:** she believes she is wanted only for being *useful*. Being useful is how she stays, how she earns a chair at every table, and how nobody can send her home.
- **The arc:** she learns she can be wanted when nothing is wrong. She discovers someone likes her company, not her services.
- **What the right answers have in common:**
  - Precise attention: you noticed the actual thing she said.
  - Honest "I don't know" over bluffing. She respects that more than anything.
  - Treating her expertise as real, without treating her as only her expertise.
- **What the well-meant wrong answers have in common** (ordinary wrong, +1):
  - Flattery: she hears it as noise.
  - Praising her *usefulness*: it feeds the fear.
  - Bluffing knowledge.
  - Trying to "take care of" her as if she were a patient.
  - Dragging her somewhere loud.
- **What the offensive answers have in common** (−1): mockery of her work as paranoid, weird or unladylike; telling her a princess shouldn't do this.

## Stats and events

- **Midpoint requirement:** Intelligence ≥ 6 and Kindness ≥ 4.
- **Optional event before the midpoint:** Intelligence ≥ 3.
- **Optional event after the midpoint:** Charisma ≥ 3.

## Things to avoid

- Anything that reads like the source work, beyond "sharp apothecary who loves poisons and is unimpressed by royalty".
- Poison as a weapon, poison on people, or self-testing.
- Her softening into sweetness. She gets warmer by getting more precise about the player.
